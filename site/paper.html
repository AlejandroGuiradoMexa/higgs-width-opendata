How wide is the Higgs boson? The four tasks of the DESY ATLAS PhD project, carried out at small scale with open data and open tools

Alejandro Guirado
Applicant, PhD position in the ATLAS group at DESY (EURAXESS 467858)

Abstract

This document accompanies my application to the PhD position in the ATLAS group at DESY. The vacancy names four tasks: the Higgs boson width from H→ZZ events, tests of the stability of the result under its assumptions, effective field theory interpretations, and a study of electron shower shapes for the authorship qualification. Each is carried out here end to end with public inputs. A four-lepton selection on 36.6 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV ATLAS Open Data gives $\mu_\text{on} = 0.93^{+0.21}_{-0.19}$ at 6.1σ. A joint on/off-shell fit with MCFM gg→ZZ templates, including the signal–continuum interference, gives $\Gamma_H < 50$ MeV at 95% CL (54 MeV expected) from $m_{4\ell}$ alone. Stress tests show that the K-factor, the continuum normalisation and the equality of on- and off-shell couplings move the result most. The off-shell tail constrains the anomalous couplings to $|a_3| < 1.1$ and $a_2 > -0.52$, where the on-shell rate alone gives no constraint. A Geant4 model of an ATLAS-like LAr barrel reproduces the shower-shape variables and their dependence on upstream material. The aim is to show each step built and checked, with its limits stated, not to approach the precision of the ATLAS measurements. All numbers are computed by code in the accompanying repository.

Rediscovering H→ZZ*→4ℓ

Prerequisite (for vacancy tasks 1–3). The off-shell rate measures $\mu_\text{on}\,\Gamma_H/\Gamma_H^\text{SM}$, so the width needs the on-shell signal strength. It is measured from four-lepton events in 36.6 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV ATLAS Open Data (2015–16, education release), with the official MC samples for signal and backgrounds.

  • Selection: trigger + matched lepton, loose/medium identification and isolation, $p_\text{T} > 20/15/10$ GeV, two same-flavour opposite-sign pairs, $Z_1$ closest to $m_Z$ with $50 < m_{12} < 106$ GeV, $m_{34}$ threshold rising from 12 to 50 GeV, $\Delta R > 0.1$, J/ψ veto.
  • Reducible background (Z+jets, tt̄) has only a handful of high-weight MC events; its shape is smoothed per sample with a kernel estimate and given a 50% normalisation uncertainty.
  • Result: $\mu_\text{on} = 0.93^{+0.21}_{-0.19}$, with 6.1σ observed and 7.2σ expected significance (asymptotic formulae); 49 events in 115–130 GeV (31 2e2μ, 13 4μ, 5 4e) against 54.4 expected.

Width from the off-shell tail

Vacancy task 1 (Higgs boson width determination using H→ZZ events). Above $2m_Z$ the gg→H*→ZZ rate scales as $\mu_\text{off} = \mu_\text{on}\,\Gamma_H/\Gamma_H^\text{SM}$, while the on-shell rate does not depend on $\Gamma_H$ [1, 2]. Comparing both constrains the width. The signal interferes destructively with the gg→ZZ continuum, so the expected yield in each $m_{4\ell}$ bin is

$$n = K\left(\mu_\text{off}\, S + \sqrt{\mu_\text{off}}\, I + B_{gg}\right) + \mu_{q\bar q}\, Q + O .$$

Templates. MCFM gg→ZZ→2e2μ at LO (cteq6l1, $\mu_R = \mu_F = m_{4\ell}/2$): processes 130 (S+I), 129 (I) and 132 ($B_{gg}$), with $S = (S+I) - I$ because process 128 returns zero above 220 GeV in this version; qq→ZZ at NLO (process 81, CT10). $K = 1.7$ multiplies S, I and $B_{gg}$, and the 4e and 4μ templates are taken equal to 2e2μ. Detector folding. Efficiency × acceptance per $m_{4\ell}$ bin is the Open Data qq→ZZ sample divided by MCFM NLO, in nine bins from 230 to 1010 GeV (522 events). Likelihood. Poisson terms for the on-shell bins of Section 2 and the off-shell bins, with $\mu_\text{off} = \mu_\text{on} R$, $R = \Gamma_H/\Gamma_H^\text{SM}$, and $\mu_\text{on}$, $R$ and $\mu_{q\bar q}$ free; intervals from $-2\Delta\ln L$ with Wilks' theorem, expected results from Asimov data at $R = 1$.

Result. $R < 12.2$ observed and $< 13.3$ expected at 95% CL, that is $\Gamma_H < 50$ MeV (54 MeV expected) for $\Gamma_H^\text{SM} = 4.1$ MeV. The best fit is at $R = 0$, with $\mu_{q\bar q} = 1.21$ relative to MCFM NLO. With the same model the expected limit becomes 7.3, 5.5 and 2.6 at 140, 300 and 3000 fb⁻¹ (statistical and $\mu_{q\bar q}$ uncertainties only).

Stability under the assumptions

Vacancy task 2 (tests of the stability of the results with respect to various assumptions). Each scenario changes one assumption of the baseline fit. Pseudo-data are generated with the baseline model at $\Gamma_H = \Gamma_H^\text{SM}$ and HL-LHC luminosity (3000 fb⁻¹), so any shift away from 1 is the bias the assumption would introduce.

K-factor. 1.4 or 2.0 instead of 1.7 moves the fitted width from 1.0 to 0.4 or 1.8: an 18% change in K shifts the width by 60–80%. With a form factor in the loop, this is the largest effect at HL-LHC luminosity, so the K-factors of S, I and B and their correlation matter as much as the data. Continuum normalisation. Constraining $\mu_{q\bar q}$ to the MC prediction within 10%, when the pseudo-data carry 1.21, pulls the width to 0.05: with $m_{4\ell}$ alone the continuum normalisation and the width are strongly correlated. New physics in the loop. A form factor $1/(1 + (m_{4\ell}/\Lambda)^2)$ on the off-shell amplitude biases the width to 1.4 for $\Lambda = 1$ TeV and 2.6 for 500 GeV.

Relaxing the on/off-shell link. If the couplings differ by a factor $c$ off shell, the fit returns $c\,\Gamma_H$ exactly (Figure 4b): the width result is a statement on this assumption as much as on $\Gamma_H$.

Effective field theory interpretation

Vacancy task 3 (effective field theory interpretations). Anomalous HZZ couplings $a_2$ (CP-even) and $a_3$ (CP-odd) correspond to dimension-6 operators of the Higgs basis. Their amplitudes grow with energy through the HZZ vertex, so the off-shell tail constrains them far better than the on-shell rate.

Method. MCFM-JHUGen with the anomalous HZZ couplings set at run time (patched reader) and $\Gamma_H = \Gamma_H^\text{SM}$; the off-shell S and I templates are interpolated quadratically in each coupling. On shell, $\sigma/\sigma_\text{SM} = 1 + 1.10\,a_2 + 0.40\,a_2^2$ and $1 + 0.149\,a_3^2$; with $\mu_F$ profiled, rates alone cannot separate a coupling from the overall gg→H strength. Off shell, the signal at $a_2 = 0.5$ ($a_3 = 0.5$) is 3.6 (2.8) times the SM in 230–1010 GeV. Result. $|a_3| < 1.1$ and $a_2 > -0.52$ at 95% CL (expected ±1.1 and −0.60), with the couplings as defined in MCFM-JHUGen and a scanned range of $[-3, 3]$.

Electron shower shapes in a LAr calorimeter

Vacancy task 4 (study of electron shower shape development; authorship qualification). A Geant4 application in C++ [6] models an ATLAS-like barrel: presampler, fine-η strips, middle and back Pb/LAr layers with ATLAS cell sizes, plus an Fe/scintillator hadronic section. 32 000 showers of e⁻, γ, π⁰ and π⁻ were simulated at 10–100 GeV.

Geometry. Periods of 1.5 mm Pb and 4.2 mm LAr ($X_0^\text{eff} \approx 19$ mm) give about 4.3, 16 and 2 $X_0$ in the strip, middle and back layers, with ATLAS cell sizes in η×φ, a presampler and an Fe/scintillator section of about 5λ. Upstream material is an aluminium slab of 0–6 $X_0$. FTFP_BERT, no magnetic field, impact at a cell centre. Calibration. Sampling fraction 0.241; electron resolution $8.4\%/\sqrt{E}$ with a constant term compatible with zero (photons $8.7\%/\sqrt{E} \oplus 0.4\%$); shower maximum at 5.2 $X_0$ for e⁻ and 6.2 $X_0$ for γ at 50 GeV from a Gamma-function fit. Material. From 0 to 6 $X_0$ upstream, $R_\eta$ of 50 GeV electrons falls from 0.969 to 0.933, $f_1$ rises from 0.10 to 0.52 and the mean reconstructed energy drops from 50.4 to 34.7 GeV: the mechanism behind the data/MC shower-shape differences that the authorship task addresses.

Code: g4calo (C++)showers.pytests

Outlook for the PhD

Each part of this study has a direct counterpart in the full analysis.

  • Width. A matrix-element or neural-network discriminant, the 2ℓ2ν channel and the VBF/EW off-shell component, with the full Run-2 and Run-3 datasets.
  • Theory inputs. Higher-order gg→ZZ K-factors for signal, continuum and interference with their uncertainties, instead of one flat factor.
  • Assumptions. Each scenario of Section 4 as an explicit nuisance parameter or alternative model in the fit, and a measurement of which ones the data can constrain.
  • EFT. SMEFT in the Warsaw basis with full interference, and angular observables to lift the $a_2$ degeneracy.
  • Shower shapes. Full ATLAS simulation and data, with data/MC corrections derived and validated on Z→ee and radiative Z decays.

Geant4 11.4.3 [5], C++17, FTFP_BERT.

Python, uproot, awkward, pyhf, iminuit, scipy, pytest; figures with ROOT 6.40 (PyROOT).
