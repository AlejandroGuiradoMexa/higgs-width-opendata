How wide is the Higgs boson? The four PhD tasks, rebuilt at small scale.

I prepared this study for the evaluation of my application to the PhD position in the ATLAS group at DESY (ref. FHDO019/2026). The vacancy lists four tasks: the Higgs boson width from H→ZZ, the stability of that result under its assumptions, EFT interpretations, and electron shower shapes for the authorship qualification. Each one is carried out here end to end with public data and tools (ATLAS Open Data, MCFM and a Geant4 calorimeter written for this purpose), so that the method, the results and the limits can be judged directly. Every number comes from code in the linked repository. ROOT in the top bar shows the ATLAS-style ROOT figures instead of the interactive plots.

0

Purpose and scope

For the evaluators. Modules B–E follow the four tasks of the vacancy in order; module A is the H→4ℓ selection and on-shell fit they all rely on. The goal is not to approach the precision of the ATLAS measurements, which use the full Run-2 dataset, multivariate discriminants and the 2ℓ2ν channel, but to show each step built and checked by me: event selection on real data, generator-level templates with interference, a profile-likelihood fit, stress tests of its assumptions, an EFT parametrisation and a detector simulation. The simplifications are stated where they enter, and section ✓ lists them together.

Built for this study

  • Four-lepton selection, background modelling and on-shell fit on ATLAS Open Data (uproot, awkward, pyhf).
  • MCFM 7.0 (JHUGen fork) compiled with gfortran 16 on Apple silicon; two legacy array declarations and a run-time reader for the anomalous HZZ couplings patched (the patch is in the repository).
  • A joint on/off-shell likelihood with signal–continuum interference, written from scratch and unit-tested.
  • A Geant4 11.4 application in C++17 with an ATLAS-like LAr barrel and the ATLAS shower-shape definitions.
  • ATLAS-style figures in ROOT; continuous integration runs the 13 unit tests on every push.

How each step would grow in the PhD

  • Width: a matrix-element or neural-network discriminant, the 2ℓ2ν channel and the VBF/EW off-shell component, with the full Run-2 and Run-3 datasets.
  • Theory inputs: higher-order gg→ZZ K-factors for signal, continuum and interference with their uncertainties, instead of one flat factor.
  • Assumptions: turn each scenario of module C into an explicit nuisance parameter or alternative model in the fit, and quantify which ones the data can constrain.
  • EFT: SMEFT in the Warsaw basis with full interference, and angular observables to lift the a2 degeneracy.
  • Shower shapes: full ATLAS simulation and data, with data/MC corrections derived and validated on Z→ee and radiative Z decays.
A

Rediscovering H→ZZ*→4ℓ

Prerequisite for tasks 1–3. The off-shell rate measures μon·ΓH/ΓHSM, so the width needs the on-shell signal strength μon. It is measured here from four-lepton events in 36.6 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV ATLAS Open Data (2015–16, education release), using the official MC samples for signal and backgrounds.

Four-lepton invariant mass

Profile likelihood for μon

Binned fit in 110–140 GeV with pyhf: Higgs signal, ZZ* and reducible backgrounds with normalisation, luminosity and MC-statistics nuisances.

  • Selection: trigger + matched lepton, loose/medium ID and isolation, pT > 20/15/10 GeV, two same-flavour opposite-sign pairs, Z1 closest to mZ with 50 < m12 < 106 GeV, m34 threshold rising from 12 to 50 GeV, ΔR > 0.1, J/ψ veto.
  • Reducible background (Z+jets, tt̄) has only a handful of high-weight MC events; its shape is smoothed per sample with a kernel estimate and given a 50% normalisation uncertainty.
  • Result: μon = 0.93 +0.21−0.19, 6.1σ observed (7.2σ expected) with the asymptotic formulae; 49 events in 115–130 GeV (31 2e2μ, 13 4μ, 5 4e) against 54.4 expected.

B

Width from the off-shell tail

Vacancy task 1: Higgs boson width determination using H→ZZ events. Above 2mZ the gg→H*→ZZ rate scales as μoff = μon·ΓH/ΓHSM, while the on-shell rate does not depend on ΓH. Comparing both constrains the width. The signal interferes destructively with the gg→ZZ continuum, so the yield is n = K(μ S + √μ I + Bgg) + μqq Q + O.

Off-shell m4ℓ as the width changes

Templates: MCFM 7.0 (JHUGen fork) gg→ZZ→2e2μ at LO with K = 1.7, folded with a per-bin efficiency calibrated on ATLAS Open Data qq→ZZ against MCFM NLO.

Likelihood scan in ΓH/ΓHSM

Expected reach vs luminosity

  • Templates: MCFM gg→ZZ→2e2μ at LO (cteq6l1, μR = μF = m4ℓ/2): processes 130 (S+I), 129 (I) and 132 (continuum Bgg), with S = (S+I) − I because process 128 returns zero above 220 GeV in this version. qq→ZZ at NLO (process 81, CT10). K = 1.7 applied to S, I and Bgg; 4e and 4μ are taken equal to 2e2μ.
  • Detector folding: efficiency × acceptance per m4ℓ bin from the Open Data qq→ZZ sample divided by MCFM NLO, in nine bins from 230 to 1010 GeV (522 events).
  • Likelihood: Poisson terms for the on-shell bins of module A and the off-shell bins, with μoff = μon·R, R = ΓH/ΓHSM, and μon, R and μqq free. Intervals from −2Δln L with Wilks; the expected results use Asimov data at R = 1.
  • Result: R < 12.2 observed, < 13.3 expected at 95% CL, i.e. ΓH < 50 MeV (54 MeV expected) with ΓHSM = 4.1 MeV. The best fit is at R = 0, and the qq→ZZ normalisation is fitted at μqq = 1.21 relative to MCFM NLO. With the same model the expected limit becomes 7.3, 5.5 and 2.6 at 140, 300 and 3000 fb⁻¹ (statistics and μqq only).

C

How stable is the result?

Vacancy task 2: tests of the stability of the results with respect to various assumptions. Each scenario changes one assumption of the baseline fit. Pseudo-data are generated with the baseline model at ΓH = ΓHSM and HL-LHC luminosity (3000 fb⁻¹), so any shift away from 1 is the bias the assumption would introduce.

Fitted width under each assumption (truth = 1)

Couplings that differ on and off shell

If (κgκZ)² off shell is c times its on-shell value (e.g. running couplings or new states in the loop), a fit assuming c = 1 returns c·ΓH.

Relaxing the on/off-shell link

Why the indirect width needs BBSM = 0

  • K-factor: 1.4 or 2.0 instead of 1.7 moves the fitted width from 1.0 to 0.4 or 1.8: an 18% change in K shifts the width by 60–80%. With a form factor in the loop, this is the largest effect at HL-LHC luminosity, so the K-factors of S, I and B and their correlation matter as much as the data.
  • qq→ZZ normalisation: constraining μqq to the MC prediction within 10%, when the pseudo-data carry 1.21, pulls the width to 0.05: with m4ℓ alone the continuum normalisation and the width are strongly correlated.
  • New physics in the ggH loop: a form factor 1/(1+(m4ℓ/Λ)²) on the off-shell amplitude biases the width to 1.4 (Λ = 1 TeV) and 2.6 (Λ = 500 GeV).
  • On/off-shell link: with couplings (κgκZ)² that differ by a factor c off shell, the fit returns c·ΓH exactly; with μoff free the width is not determined at all. The width result is a result on this assumption as much as on ΓH.

D

EFT interpretation

Vacancy task 3: effective field theory interpretations. Anomalous HZZ couplings a2 (CP-even) and a3 (CP-odd) correspond to dimension-6 operators of the Higgs basis. Their amplitudes grow with the energy through the HZZ vertex, so the off-shell tail constrains them far better than the on-shell rate.

Off-shell signal for different a2

Constraints: on-shell only vs on + off shell

With rates only, the on-shell measurement cannot separate a coupling change from the overall gg→H strength μF (its curve stays at zero). The off-shell tail breaks that degeneracy. For a₂ > 0 a second degeneracy remains: the larger signal is cancelled by the stronger destructive interference, which angular observables would resolve.

  • Generation: MCFM-JHUGen with the anomalous HZZ couplings set at run time (patched reader), ΓH = ΓHSM; off-shell S and I templates are interpolated quadratically in each coupling from runs at several values.
  • On-shell rate: σ/σSM = 1 + 1.10 a2 + 0.40 a2² and 1 + 0.149 a3². With μF profiled, rates alone cannot separate a coupling from the overall gg→H strength.
  • Off shell: the signal at a2 = 0.5 (a3 = 0.5) is 3.6 (2.8) times the SM in 230–1010 GeV.
  • Result: |a3| < 1.1 and a2 > −0.52 at 95% CL (expected ±1.1 and −0.60), with the couplings as defined in MCFM-JHUGen and a scanned range of [−3, 3].

E

Electron shower shapes in a LAr calorimeter

Vacancy task 4: study of electron shower shape development (authorship qualification). A Geant4 application in C++ models an ATLAS-like barrel: presampler, fine-η strips, middle and back Pb/LAr layers with ATLAS cell sizes, plus an Fe/scintillator hadronic section. 24 000 showers of e⁻, γ, π⁰ and π⁻ were simulated at 10–100 GeV.

The simulated detector

Side view of the Geant4 geometry. The electromagnetic layers are drawn to scale in radiation lengths and each line is one Pb/LAr period; the upstream material and the hadronic section are not to scale. Particles enter at a cell centre, without magnetic field.

Single simulated showers

The first five events of each sample, not selected. Each map is the readout window of one layer in the same η×φ frame, so the change of granularity from layer to layer is visible. The two photons of a π⁰ can leave two separate maxima in the strips (π⁰ events 2 and 4), which Eratio and ws,tot exploit. A π⁻ either crosses the LAr layers with little energy and showers in the hadronic section (events 3 and 4, Rhad) or starts its shower early. The strips collect about 10% of the visible energy of a 50 GeV electron without upstream material and about half of it behind 6 X0.

One event drawn by Geant4

The geometry and the particle trajectories of single events, exported by the VRML driver of Geant4 from the same application and shown in 3D. Electrons in red, positrons in blue, photons in green; one event per sample, not selected. The electromagnetic shower develops in the Pb/LAr layers. The π⁻ shower is longer and reaches the hadronic section; switch on “other” to see its hadrons.

Shower-shape distributions at 50 GeV

Material in front of the calorimeter

Upstream material (inner detector, solenoid, cryostat) starts showers early. This is the main source of data/MC differences in shower shapes.

Particle identification power (25 GeV)

Longitudinal profile and resolution

  • Geometry: 1.5 mm Pb + 4.2 mm LAr periods (X0eff ≈ 19 mm), 4.5, 16.1 and 2.1 X0 in the strip, middle and back layers, ATLAS cell sizes in η×φ, a presampler, and an Fe/scintillator section of about 5 λ. Upstream material is an aluminium slab of 0–6 X0. FTFP_BERT, no magnetic field, impact at a cell centre.
  • Calibration: sampling fraction 0.241. Electron resolution 8.4%/√E with a constant term compatible with zero (photons 8.7%/√E ⊕ 0.4%); shower maximum at 5.2 X0 for e⁻ and 6.2 X0 for γ at 50 GeV, from a Gamma-function fit.
  • Material: from 0 to 6 X0 upstream, Rη of 50 GeV electrons falls from 0.969 to 0.933, f1 rises from 0.10 to 0.52 and the mean reconstructed energy drops from 50.4 to 34.7 GeV. This is the mechanism behind the data/MC shower-shape differences that the authorship task addresses.
  • Identification: Eratio and ws,tot in the strips separate γ from π⁰; Rhad and E/p separate e⁻ from π⁻.
